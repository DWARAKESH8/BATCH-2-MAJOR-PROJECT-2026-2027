Control Room Alert and Broadcast Templates

Structure of an internal alert. Every alert passed to a supervisor or marshal must contain, in
this order: severity, location, the measurement that triggered it, the suspected mechanism, the
recommended action, the time available, and a reference number for acknowledgement.

Worked example.
  SEVERITY: Alert
  LOCATION: Gate 3 throat
  MEASUREMENT: peak local density 4.3 persons per square metre, throughput down 40 percent over
    ninety seconds, counter-flow present
  MECHANISM: bottleneck congestion with opposing stream from the closed east exit
  ACTION: meter inflow at Gate 3 approach; open Gate 4 as diversion; two marshals to the throat
  TIME AVAILABLE: critical density projected in approximately 90 seconds on current trend;
    opening a diversion route takes about 120 seconds to take effect, so act now
  REFERENCE: A-4417

Acknowledgement. The receiving supervisor must acknowledge with the reference number, state what
action they are taking, and state their location. An unacknowledged alert is an open alert and
must be repeated to the next person in the chain, not simply logged.

Public address principles.
- One instruction per announcement. Two instructions halve compliance.
- Name the destination explicitly. "Move to Gate 4" not "use an alternative exit".
- Say what people will find there, so they believe the instruction is worth following.
- Repeat identical wording. Changed wording is heard as new information and causes hesitation.
- Never say the words "panic", "crush" or "emergency" to a dense crowd unless evacuation is
  actually being ordered.
- Address behaviour that helps: stop pushing forward, keep arms at chest height, help anyone
  who falls, keep moving steadily.

Example broadcasts.
Congestion: "Gate 4 is now open and there is plenty of room. Please follow the marshals to your
left and keep moving steadily."
Counter-flow: "This walkway is now one-way. If you are heading toward the north stand, please
use the outer route signposted by staff."
Severe compression: "Everyone please stop pushing forward. Keep your arms up at chest height.
Staff are opening the barriers ahead of you. Do not turn around."

Recording obligations. Log the time each alert was raised, the time it was acknowledged, who
acknowledged it, what action was taken, and the time the action took effect. These timestamps
are the primary evidence in any subsequent review, and reconstructing them afterwards from
memory is not possible.
