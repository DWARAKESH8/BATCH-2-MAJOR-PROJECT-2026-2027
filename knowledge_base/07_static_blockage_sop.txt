Static Blockage and Crowd Standstill Standard Operating Procedure

What it is. A dense crowd that has stopped moving while people continue to arrive behind it.
The crowd is not turbulent, not panicked, and frequently entirely calm. It is simply stuck.
This is the state immediately preceding a crush, and it is the last point at which
intervention is straightforward.

Recognition. High density combined with near-zero movement, low directional disorder, and a
broad stopped front rather than a funnel. Throughput is effectively zero. The distinguishing
feature against a bottleneck is shape: a bottleneck squeezes people into a narrow column at an
aperture, whereas a blockage stops a wide front where it stands. The distinguishing feature
against turbulence is motion: turbulence moves violently, a blockage does not move at all.

Why it is dangerous. Arrivals do not stop simply because movement has. Every second of
standstill adds people to the back of a crowd that cannot absorb them, and the resulting
pressure has no release path. The danger grows with time held, not with the instantaneous
reading, which is why the duration of the standstill matters more than its density.

Immediate actions, in order.
1. Stop inflow at every upstream decision point immediately. This is the only action that
   addresses the cause.
2. Find and clear the obstruction at the head of the standstill. Blockages usually have a
   specific, physical, removable cause: a closed door, a fallen barrier, a medical incident, a
   stalled vehicle, a queue check that has stopped, a locked gate nobody has reported.
3. Open a release path in the direction the crowd is already facing. People will move forward
   readily and will resist being turned.
4. Send a marshal physically to the head of the crowd to report what is actually there. Camera
   views frequently do not show the obstruction, and the control room's assumption about the
   cause is often wrong.
5. If the crowd has not begun moving within sixty seconds, escalate to the crush response.

Prohibited actions. Do not broadcast any message that causes people to turn around or move
back. Reversing a jammed crowd creates opposing pressure inside the jam, and the people at the
interface have nowhere to go. Do not add marshals into the body of the crowd; work the edges
and the head.
