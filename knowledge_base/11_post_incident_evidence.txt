Post-Event Records and Incident Evidence Procedure

Purpose. Two distinct purposes, and both matter. The first is learning: aggregated records
across many events show which gates chronically bottleneck, at what time, and under what
conditions, and that evidence justifies permanent changes to the physical layout in a way that
individual recollections never do. The second is accountability: every public inquiry into a
crowd disaster has had to establish who knew what, and when. Reconstructing that from memory
after the event is not possible.

What must be retained for every event.
- The full time series of measurements per zone: occupancy, local density, movement speed,
  directional coherence, throughput and crowd pressure.
- Every alert raised, with its severity, zone, triggering measurement, and timestamp.
- Every acknowledgement, with the identity of the person who acknowledged it, what action they
  reported taking, and the time of that acknowledgement.
- The recommended action generated for each alert, together with the source documents it was
  drawn from, so that the basis of the advice can be examined afterwards.
- Any alert that was never acknowledged. These are the most important records in the set.
- The calibration state of each camera during the event, since it conditions every density
  figure reported.

What to review after every event, not only after incidents.
- The highest density reached in each zone and how long it was held.
- The interval between the first alert for an incident and the first acknowledgement.
- The interval between acknowledgement and the action taking physical effect.
- Any alert judged a false alarm, and why. A high false-alarm rate is a safety problem in its
  own right, because it trains operators to disregard the system.
- Zones that recur across events. A gate that reaches four persons per square metre at every
  event is a design fault, not an operational one, and no amount of marshalling will fix it.

Privacy. Retain measurements, not identities. The system records how many people were in a zone
and how they moved, and does not perform facial recognition or identify individuals. Retention
of the underlying video should follow the venue's existing policy and should not be extended
merely because analysis was run over it.

Data quality caveats to record alongside the numbers. Note periods of heavy detector occlusion,
where counts should be read as lower bounds; periods when a camera was uncalibrated, where
densities are relative rather than absolute; and any camera outage, since an absence of alerts
from an unmonitored zone is not evidence that the zone was safe.
