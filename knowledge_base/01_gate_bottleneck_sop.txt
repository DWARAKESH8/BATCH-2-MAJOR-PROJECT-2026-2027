Gate and Chokepoint Bottleneck Standard Operating Procedure

Scope. Applies to entries, exits, turnstiles, stairways, bridges, tunnels, ramps and any
constriction where the walkway narrows and arrival rate can exceed discharge rate.

Recognition. A bottleneck is forming when the queue upstream of the constriction grows while
the number of people passing through per minute stops rising or begins to fall. This is the
congested branch of the pedestrian flow relationship: beyond a critical density, adding more
people reduces total throughput rather than increasing it. Crowd density at the throat rising
above three persons per square metre with falling throughput is the operational trigger.

Immediate actions, in order.
1. Meter the inflow upstream. Hold arriving groups in batches at the previous decision point.
   This is the single highest-value action and it becomes harder every second it is delayed.
2. Open additional discharge capacity. Every extra metre of effective aperture width increases
   discharge roughly in proportion, so opening a second gate is worth far more than urging
   people at the existing one to move faster.
3. Send marshals to the throat to keep people moving through it and to stop anybody halting,
   turning, or setting objects down inside the aperture.
4. Inspect the aperture for a physical cause. Bottlenecks frequently have a removable cause:
   a stalled wheelchair, a merchandise stand, a fallen barrier, a ticket check that has slowed,
   a person stopped to use a phone. Clearing the cause restores throughput instantly.
5. Broadcast a route diversion naming the alternative gate explicitly and telling people what
   they will find there.

Prohibited actions. Do not close a gate that a crowd is already pressing toward. Closing the
discharge point of a loaded queue converts congestion into compression within seconds. Reduce
the flow upstream instead and let the queue drain forward.

Escalation. If throughput has not recovered within two minutes of metering, or if density at
the throat exceeds five persons per square metre, escalate to the crush response procedure and
notify the safety officer directly rather than through the routine channel.

Recording. Log the time of first recognition, the time inflow metering began, the identity of
the gate opened as an alternative, and the throughput before and after. These four facts are
the ones asked for in every post-event review.
