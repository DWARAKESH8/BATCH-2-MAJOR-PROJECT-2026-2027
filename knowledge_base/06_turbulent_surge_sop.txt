Crowd Turbulence and Stop-and-Go Surge Standard Operating Procedure

What it is. At high density a crowd stops behaving like a collection of individuals and starts
behaving like a compressible medium. Pressure applied at one point propagates through contact,
producing shock waves that travel backward through the crowd. People are displaced laterally
without choosing to move and without being able to resist. This regime is called crowd
turbulence, and its onset is measured as local density multiplied by the variance of local
velocities, a quantity that rises sharply before any visible catastrophe.

Recognition. The crowd surges forward, stalls, and surges again on a cycle of a few seconds.
Individuals are pushed sideways as well as forward. Density is high and irregular rather than
uniform. Arms and objects appear above the crowd surface. Sound changes: the crowd becomes
either markedly louder or abruptly quieter.

This is the mechanism behind the most lethal recorded crowd incidents. It appears at densities
that a casual observer would describe merely as very crowded, and it is not preceded by panic.

Immediate actions, in order.
1. Stop all inflow to the affected block immediately. Not reduce; stop.
2. Open every available egress route out of the block, including routes normally reserved.
3. Insert marshals from the DOWNSTREAM side to break the wave laterally in sections. Sending
   responders against the flow puts them inside the wave and removes them as responders.
4. Give one clear, calm instruction over public address and repeat it unchanged. Conflicting
   messages cause parts of the crowd to change direction, which amplifies the wave.
5. Stage medical resources at the densest edge before casualties are reported, not after.
6. Where a stage, screen or attraction is drawing the crowd forward, stop the attraction. A
   pause in the show removes the driving force more effectively than any barrier.

Prohibited actions. Do not narrow or close exits in an attempt to control the flow. Do not
attempt to move responders upstream through the crowd. Do not instruct the crowd to turn around.

Escalation. Turbulence is a major incident by definition. Notify the safety officer, medical
lead and, where the scale warrants it, external emergency services at the point of recognition
rather than after casualties appear.
