Inflow Metering and Early Intervention Standard Operating Procedure

Principle. Every crowd-safety intervention has a latency between the decision and its physical
effect. Closing or opening a gate takes roughly a minute. Establishing a diversion route takes
around two minutes. Walking marshals into position takes around three. A public address
announcement changes behaviour in about thirty seconds, and only for the fraction of the crowd
that hears and believes it. Meanwhile a crowd can pass from crowded to critical in under a
minute. It follows that an intervention triggered when the situation is already dangerous
cannot arrive in time, and the only interventions that work are the ones started on the trend.

When to meter. Begin metering inflow when occupancy is rising steadily and local density is
approaching three persons per square metre, even though nothing yet looks wrong. This is the
cheapest intervention available and its cost rises steeply with delay: metering a queue of two
hundred is routine, metering a queue of two thousand is a public order problem.

How to meter.
1. Meter at the furthest upstream decision point that still has holding space, not at the
   constriction itself. Holding people where there is room is the entire technique.
2. Release in batches sized to the discharge capacity of the constriction downstream, with a
   pause between batches. Continuous trickle release causes people to bunch at the aperture.
3. Tell the held crowd what is happening and roughly how long. An unexplained hold generates
   pushing; an explained hold generally does not.
4. Keep counting. Metering without measuring the effect is guesswork.
5. Stop metering as soon as downstream throughput recovers. Holding longer than necessary
   builds a second queue that will itself need managing.

Pre-conditions to have ready before an event. Nominate the metering points and the holding
areas in advance and record their capacity. Agree the trigger levels with the safety officer
before the event, in writing, so that no argument about thresholds is required during it.
Confirm the diversion routes are physically open and staffed. Confirm who has authority to stop
inflow and confirm they can be reached in under thirty seconds.

Recording. Log the trigger measurement, the decision time, the time the first batch was held,
and the downstream throughput before and after. If throughput does not recover within two
minutes, the constriction has a physical cause that metering will not fix, and it must be
inspected directly.
